Don't Panic

Printed, as such things traditionally are, in large friendly letters. What follows is a rather less reassuring rundown of the actual galaxy: who runs it, who's shooting at whom, and which planets will simply let you suffocate if you forget to check. Bring a spacesuit. Possibly a towel. Use the search bar above to jump straight to what you need.

Home planet, abandoned

Humanity did not leave Earth out of ambition. In 2150 scientists confirmed the planet's magnetosphere was failing, and while trying to work out why, they made it worse: gravity-warping experiments meant to unlock deep-space travel instead stripped away what atmosphere Earth had left. The grav drive that resulted is the only reason anyone is reading this in another star system at all. Earth itself was fully evacuated by 2203 and now sits in the Sol system as a cold, irradiated reminder of the trade humanity made — visitable, mineable, and dead.

What rebuilt itself out among the stars split cleanly in two. The United Colonies is the closest thing to a proper government: bureaucratic, well-armed, and convinced it's the adult in the room. The Freestar Collective is the frontier answer to that — a looser alliance of independent colonies that would rather handle its own problems, thanks. The two fought a war over it once. They have not entirely stopped disagreeing.

Everyone else — explorers, pirates, zealots, and one deeply enthusiastic drinks-and-drugs megacorporation — operates in the gaps between the two. That's most of this guide.

§2

State of Play

The galaxy keeps moving even after you've stopped playing. Here's what's changed recently, straight from the official record.

Free Lanes

Free update7 April 2026

The biggest free update yet, released alongside the official PlayStation 5 launch. Cruise Mode now lets you walk around your ship while covering huge in-system distances instead of jump-cutting past them. A new material, X-Tech, unlocks higher-tier weapon, gear and ship modifications and lets you reroll legendary effects. Outposts share containers with each other for easier inventory management, and Starborn players can now rank up their powers by collecting Quantum Essence — no New Game Plus required.

  • New travel hub: Anchorpoint Station, Algorab system (see Frontier)
  • New vehicle: the Moon Jumper
  • A five-million-credit asteroid mansion, if you've been saving

Terran Armada

Story DLC7 April 2026

A new hostile faction: military deserters who've built a robot army with one goal, uniting humanity by force. The story starts with a hijacked luxury liner, the Opulence of the Stars, in the Nirvana system — recommended level 50+. Once triggered, Incursions can break out anywhere: Terran Armada forces storm an existing location and turn it into a live battle, disabling fast travel until you've cleared them out. How often they happen is adjustable in the gameplay options.

Shattered Space

Story expansion2024 · ongoing

Starfield's first story expansion, and the deepest the game gets into House Va'ruun. A distress signal leads to Va'ruun'kai itself, where a botched grav-drive experiment has torn a hole in the fabric of spacetime above Dazra. Fifty-plus new locations, zero-gravity Vortex zones, and the Scaled Citadel — the sect's seat of government, now sitting inside an active energy field.

§3

Who's Actually In Charge

Eight organisations worth knowing before someone starts shooting at you and you'd like to know why.

Explorers

Constellation

The last of the great explorers' guilds — self-described "NASA-punk" idealists who care more about the unknown than the flag on their ship. Based out of the Lodge in New Atlantis. Not a government, not an army, and frequently at odds with both. You join automatically, one way or another.

Government

United Colonies

The largest and most powerful government in the settled systems, run from the MAST building in New Atlantis. Order, military structure, and a genuine belief they're holding civilisation together. Its armed wing, UC Vanguard, deals with the things it would rather the public not think about.

Confederation

Freestar Collective

Frontier colonies who fought a war rather than answer to the United Colonies, and still run things their own way from Akila City. Law enforcement is handled by the Freestar Rangers — fewer regulations, more boots on the ground.

Pirates

Crimson Fleet

Operates out of The Key, a converted former UC military station, and preys on shipping across the frontier. Recruitment runs through undercover UC work, or simply getting caught committing enough crimes to be noticed.

Megacorp

Ryujin Industries

Headquartered in Neon, the city it effectively owns. "Prepared to do anything to boost their profits" is the polite way of putting it. Recruitment is as simple as walking up to a terminal — everyone gets an interview.

Religious sect

House Va'ruun

Zealots who worship an entity called the Great Serpent, after a colony-ship passenger reported an encounter with it during a grav jump generations ago — whether that was contact or breakdown is left deliberately unresolved. Mostly encountered as hostiles from their capital, Dazra.

Mercenaries

Ecliptic

Private military contractors for hire, found wherever the pay is good and the questions are few — fortified outposts like Vulture's Roost are typical. Not an organisation you join so much as one you keep surviving.

New · 2026

Terran Armada

The newest and least reasonable arrival: military deserters who've decided the way to unite humanity is a robot army and no debate. First encountered via a hijacked cruise liner in the Nirvana system. Expect Incursions wherever you've just gotten comfortable.

§4

The Core Worlds

Four systems where most of civilisation actually lives. Everything else is, to a first approximation, rock.

Alpha Centauri Planet: Jemison
4.3 ly from Sol

New Atlantis

The biggest city in the settled systems and the UC capital, built in tiers around the MAST government complex. Immaculate, prosperous, and just self-satisfied enough that you'll understand why the Freestar Collective left. Constellation's headquarters, the Lodge, sits inside it — so this is also where your own story starts, one way or another.

Above the planet floats The Eye, an orbital station worth a look for its mission board and passing trade, if nothing else.

Sol Planets: Earth, Mars
Humanity's original address

Earth & Cydonia

Earth is still there. It's just not somewhere anyone lives — magnetosphere gone, radiation punishing by day, continents barely visible from orbit under a thin haze. You can land, salvage, and build outposts on the birthplace of the species. Nobody's stopping you. Nobody's helping you either.

Mars fares better, in the sense that Cydonia exists: a UC mining settlement sealed entirely underground in a maze of tunnels, because the surface still isn't an option. It's the largest mining operation the UC runs, guarded by its own Marine barracks, and about as far from New Atlantis's polish as the core worlds get.

Cheyenne Planet: Akila
Frontier capital

Akila City

A walled frontier town that happens to be the capital of an interstellar confederation — dusty streets, practical architecture, and a "handle it yourself" attitude baked into every district: Midtown, the Core, the Stretch. Home to the Freestar Rangers and, fittingly, the closest thing the settled systems have to a Wild West.

Volii Planet: Volii Alpha
Vice capital

Neon

Built on top of an old fishing platform, after someone noticed the local fish produced a rather profitable psychotropic compound. That compound is Aurora, and Neon is the city it built: casinos, corporate towers, and a lot of very bright light hiding a lot of very murky business. Ryujin Industries runs it, more or less openly, from Neon Core.

Out on the Frontier

The other hundred-plus systems are mostly uncharted rock, ice, and gas — but a fair few now have something worth the fuel.

Vectera & Kreet

Narion
Argos Extractors

Anselon's two moons, and where it all begins: a mining contract on Vectera, an artifact nobody expected, and a detour past Kreet on the way to New Atlantis.

The Key

Kryx
Crimson Fleet HQ

A former UC military base orbiting Suvorov, repurposed as pirate headquarters. Command decks, ship services, and a lot of people who'd rather not see UC colours.

Paradiso & Red Mile

Porrima
Resort & blood-sport

A luxury resort on Porrima II and, one planet over on Porrima III, the Red Mile — an extreme racing club that treats "extreme" fairly literally.

Dazra

Kavnyk
Va'ruun capital

The sect's seat of power on Va'ruun'kai. Outsiders are not exactly welcomed, and since the events of Shattered Space, part of the sky above it doesn't behave either.

Londinion

Toliman
Abandoned settlement

A former United Colonies town on Toliman II, left behind and now mostly notable for why nobody came back.

HopeTown

Valo
HopeTech

A settlement on Polvo built by industrialist Ron Hope, better known for the HopeTech shipyard than the town around it. Also home to the Freestar Rangers' headquarters, if you're after both a ship refit and a badge.

The Den

Wolf
Neutral ground

No faction runs it, deliberately, and the black market trades accordingly. Bring credits, not questions.

Lair of the Mantis

Denebola
Legend, mostly true

A hidden base on Denebola I-b belonging to a self-styled masked vigilante. Rumour and armour, in roughly equal parts.

Vulture's Roost

Jaffa IV
Ecliptic stronghold

A heavily fortified mercenary outpost guarding whatever they've been paid to guard. Exactly as friendly as that sounds — go in expecting a fight, not a chat.

Anchorpoint Station

Algorab
New · Free Lanes

A newly opened starstation out in the far reaches, added with the Free Lanes update. New vendors, new quests, and reportedly a safe worth cracking if you enjoy a puzzle. Needs a jump range of 27.25 ly or better to reach.

Nirvana

Nirvana system
Terran Armada

A fair distance out of the central region, and now the opening scene of the Terran Armada story: the hijacked liner Opulence of the Stars. Recommended level 50+. Don't wander in early out of curiosity.

§6

The Bit Nobody Warns You About spoilers

This is where the pocket guide stops being about geography and starts being about why the universe is bigger than the map suggests.

The Artifacts
Ancient, alien-adjacent objects scattered across dozens of systems. Recover one and you get a vision, not an explanation — the explanations come later, and cost more.
The Armillary
A device built to hold the artifacts together. Complete it, and it doesn't just decode them — it opens a way through.
The Unity
What's on the other side: not a place so much as a threshold between parallel versions of the same universe. Nobody, including its makers, is named or explained.
The Starborn
People who've crossed through the Unity, sometimes more than once — including, eventually, you. Not a faction, not an ideology: just individuals who've each had the same impossible experience and drawn very different conclusions from it. The Hunter and the Emissary, two of the oldest, disagree pointedly about who deserves to follow. As of the Free Lanes update, you no longer need to start over to grow stronger as one — Quantum Essence, collected in the world, ranks up your powers directly.
What Happens Next
You go through. Part of you stays, shaping the universe you leave behind; part of you wakes up in another one, similar but not identical, carrying what you learned. Which is a fairly elaborate way of saying: you get to do it all again, slightly wiser, in a galaxy that's rearranged itself just enough to keep you honest.

§7

Field Notes

Grav jumps follow mapped lanes, not straight lines. You can't point your ship at a star and go — the route has to exist first.

Oxygen kills faster than damage does. Extreme heat, cold and low pressure drain it before you've noticed you're in trouble. Check the planet's stats before you get out and admire the view.

Don't fly into a city hot. Contraband scanners at the gates mean anything you're smuggling gets found before you do anything with it.

Your scanner is doing more than you think. Surveying, resources, outposts — most of the economy runs through the same tool on your wrist.

Companions have opinions, and they will share them. Whoever you bring along is watching what you do, not just what you say.

Cruise Mode is worth getting used to. Since Free Lanes, it lets you walk your ship's corridors while covering the distance a loading screen used to hide. Slower, but you stop missing things.

Keep an eye on your Incursion Chance setting. If Terran Armada turning your favourite outpost into a firefight sounds fun, turn it up. If it doesn't, turn it down — it's in Gameplay Options.

A towel remains, whatever the century, about the most massively useful thing a traveller can carry. This guide cannot get you one. That's on you.
